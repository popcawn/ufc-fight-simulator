How the odds calculator works

Type a sportsbook's American odds (e.g. -150 or +220) for any outcome. The calculator anchors to the betting market: it blends the moneyline you enter with the model, weighted by when you are betting (the model moves an opening line more than a fight-week one). In a 2021–26 backtest against real opening/closing lines, raw model edges lost money while the anchored blend made money. Fair % = the probability behind fair odds & EV — the raw model until you enter both moneylines, then the market-anchored blend (shown in gold) · Mkt % = the book's no-vig price. Green = positive EV, ★ = best single bet, ⚠ suspect = the verdict says pass.

Honesty note: no fight model hits 90% — betting favorites win roughly 65–69% of UFC fights. On its own this model picks ~66% of winners on 741 held-out fights (Jan 2025 → now, pre-fight data only, no leakage), but the closing betting line is sharper still. What the backtest shows is that the model knows things the opening line doesn't — the line moves toward it before fight night — and that anchoring the model to the market is what turns that into a betting edge. That's why the calculator anchors to the line. Every number here is walk-forward: each prediction made before the fight, with the model refit only on earlier years. Past results don't guarantee future ones — expect losing cards.
